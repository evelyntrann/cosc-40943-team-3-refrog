_**What this file is.** Your script going in, your meeting record coming out. Copy it once per meeting into `docs/requirements/` as `client-interview-YYYY-MM-DD.md` and commit it the same day._

_**How to use it.** Each section says what it is for, what it is worth in minutes, and whether it must happen in this meeting. The example questions are written for a different domain (technical recruiting) on purpose, so you cannot use them unchanged. Rewrite them in your client's words before you walk in, and write the answers underneath in **their** words rather than yours._

_**Work it with your agent.** Give it your one-page brief, this file **including these instructions**, and a role: "You are an experienced business analyst preparing for a first client interview. Using the question types in this guide, write the version of each question that fits this client's domain, and list every acronym in the brief you would want defined before the meeting." Then do the part it cannot: pick which of its questions are worth your client's limited hour. **Sort by what it costs you to stay wrong**, not by what is easy to ask. Anything you could answer by reading a document is not worth a client minute._

## Listen before you build

**An idea you propose in the first twenty minutes is not worth what it costs you.** Your strongest instinct will be to show your client you understood by describing what you would build. Early, that ends the elicitation: a client who has heard your idea reacts to it instead of describing their world.

This is about order, not silence. Some clients want to think out loud with you, and a few asked for the project because they want exactly that help. Do it **after** the read-back in section 14, when you can describe their process back to them accurately and the brainstorm is grounded in their world rather than your imagination. If they open by asking for your ideas, say you have some and would rather earn them by understanding the process first, then come back to it before you leave.

**The separate rule is absolute: commit to nothing.** Not a deadline, not a feature, not "sure, we can add that". Five teammates are not in the room. "Let me write that down and bring it back to the team" is the whole sentence, and it holds even when a client pushes.

## The shape of the hour

Most first meetings run 60 to 90 minutes. Budget for the short one.

| Part | Sections | 60 min | 90 min |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening | 1 | 5 | 5 |
| The business | 2, 3 | 10 | 15 |
| The process | 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 | 25 | 40 |
| The boundaries | 9, 10, 11, 12 | 10 | 15 |
| The close | 13, 14, 15 | 10 | 15 |

**Extra time goes into section 4 first.** It repays another ten minutes and it is the only section you cannot reconstruct from notes afterward.

"Can wait" means the next meeting, not never. When you are behind, drop from the middle. **Never drop 14 or 15:** the read-back is where you learn you misunderstood something, and the close is where you stop losing two weeks to scheduling.

# The business

## 2. Context and domain

_**Must ask. 5 min.** You are here for vocabulary as much as facts. Every term you do not recognize goes in the glossary before you leave. When your client says "cycle" in one sentence and "sprint" in the next, ask which they mean while they are still in front of you; an agent reading the transcript afterward cannot ask._

_Adapt: Give us some background on refrog. Who else is involved? Any documents, slides, or videos that would get us up to speed on the terminology?

**What they said:**

ReFrog began with three locations and has grown incrementally to seven; the next event is planned for eight locations. A small, close-knit group plans the event throughout the year: Wendy, Eric, and Courtney are the core organizers, with other returning participants and some student representation. The main event runs Monday through Saturday of finals week. The team also works with donation partners: the Wellman Project takes items useful for teachers, TRIO receives business-professional clothing for its fall pop-up shop, and Archway/Very Good Buys receives remaining usable items. Wendy offered to share the Google Forms and to ask Courtney about providing a representative SignUpGenius schedule.

**Terms for the glossary, in their words:**

- **ReFrog:** A TCU move-out reuse event intended to keep usable items out of the landfill and give them to people who can use them.
- **Capsule:** A pod-like physical location, donated for ReFrog's use by DFW Capsule, where items are donated, organized, and shopped.
- **Donation partners:** Organizations that collect items ReFrog cannot keep after shopping, such as the Wellman Project and Archway.
- **Shopping:** The in-person, free selection of donated items by people at a ReFrog location; it is not an online store.

_Expect to miss it here. Baselines surface in section 4, when they are looking at the thing that takes the time. Ask the objective now, listen for the number all hour, and close the gap in the read-back._

_Adapt: Did you want to have a mobile application or a web based application?

**What they said:**

Wendy wants the app to make participation easy for people interacting with ReFrog and to put information in one place, preferably on a phone. The first feature needed is volunteer sign-up because recruiting starts about one to one and a half months before ReFrog. During the event, the app should also help with location information, item and activity data, and checking whether shoppers are part of the TCU community. Wendy was open to the team determining whether a mobile app, website, or another implementation best supports those needs.

**Candidate objectives (`BO-<slug>`), with baselines where you got them:**

- `BO-data-accuracy`: Reduce error in ReFrog's event data; baseline unknown because current counts are estimates from short Google Forms and manual observations.
- `BO-volunteer-coverage`: Make it easier to fill volunteer shifts and respond to cancellations; baseline unknown.
- `BO-waste-diversion`: Grow ReFrog's ability to keep usable materials out of landfills; 2025 estimates were 2,573 shoppers, 9,600 items found, about 89 eight-yard dumpsters diverted, and 21.36 tons.

# The process

## 4. How it works today

_**Must ask. 10 min, the best ten in the meeting.** Ask them to show you rather than tell you. People describe the process they believe they follow; the spreadsheet shows the one they actually follow, and the gap is where the requirements hide. **"Show me" is the two most productive words in requirements engineering, and they cost nothing.**_

_Walk one real recent case end to end. "Take me through the last one you did" beats "how does it usually work", because the general shape is a summary they have given before and the last real one has the exceptions in it._

_Adapt: What are the steps end-to-end on a refrog event? Could you show me your volunteer sign up form, and how you track items? Who does what at each step?_

**What they said:**

1. Throughout the year, the organizing committee meets to plan the next ReFrog event, including locations and process changes.
2. About a month before the event, Courtney recruits volunteers through email and SignUpGenius; organizers manually cover cancelled shifts or try to find replacements.
3. Organizers arrange donation-partner pickups, prepare signage and hours, and communicate event information.
4. During finals week, ReFrog operates Monday through Saturday. Donors, usually students and sometimes their families, bring items to a location.
5. Donors scan a QR code and complete a short Google Form with the date, location, and estimated number of items donated.
6. Shoppers visit locations in person, show a TCU ID, and select free items. They use a separate Google Form to record date, location, and number of items taken.
7. Volunteers greet donors, help receive and organize items, remind people to complete the forms, check shopper IDs, manage crowd flow, maintain safe and orderly capsules, and sometimes discourage excessive taking.
8. Beginning around Thursday, donation partners pick up items that remain after shopping. The final pickup occurs after the event.
9. Organizers debrief and estimate overall volume, dumpster diversion, and tonnage from item counts and observed truckloads.

**Artifacts they showed us:**

- Current donor and shopper Google Forms, offered for sharing.
- Current volunteer SignUpGenius structure, which Wendy will ask Courtney to make available in a representative form.
- Existing ReFrog website and its location map.
- Volunteer training email and video, which Wendy offered to forward.

## 5. What is hard about it

_**Must ask. 5 min.** The complaint is usually the requirement. Listen for "must", "unless", "only", and "except", which arrive unannounced in the middle of a story about something else. Those sentences are business rules, and they exist whether or not your software does._

_Adapt:  Where does it get stuck? What do you do when it goes wrong?
What is the most frustrating part about your current system? Walk me through the last time it went badly. 

**What they said:**

Volunteer scheduling is more manual than Wendy would like, especially when cancellations happen close to an event; there are hundreds of volunteer hours to cover, and organizers currently step in themselves. Current data collection has an unknown amount of error because donors and shoppers self-report only an estimated item count through QR-code forms. Organizers also cannot easily identify non-TCU shoppers, repeat shoppers, or people who may be reselling items. They want to avoid becoming "the police," but would like administrators to be able to review potential problems overnight. Donation-partner timing is another challenge: pickups need to happen early enough during move-out, and Archway did not find as many usable items as expected in its first year.

**Rules heard (candidate `BR-*` for week 4):**

- `BR-tcu-shopping-eligibility`: Shopping is intended for people with a TCU ID, including students, faculty, and staff.
- `BR-free-items`: ReFrog items are free to shoppers.
- `BR-donation-ease`: Donating must remain quick and easy; making donation too difficult may lead people to throw usable items away instead.
- `BR-in-person-shopping`: Shopping occurs in person at staffed ReFrog locations.
- `BR-volunteer-coverage`: Locations should not be staffed by only one volunteer because locations can be busy.

## 6. What already works

_**Must ask. 3 min.** Ask what is good before you propose replacing it. A team that removes something the client liked has lost trust it will not get back this semester, and nobody volunteers this unasked._

_Adapt: Is there anything you like about reFrog currently that you wouldn't want to change? Has anything been tried before that did not work, and why?_

**What they said:**

Wendy likes ReFrog's overall structure and mission and would rather grow it than shrink it. The event should continue to make donating easy, keep usable items out of landfills, and get them to people who can use them. She said the team starts small and grows incrementally rather than letting changes get out of control. Individual item categorization would be useful only if it is very fast and easy for donors; the current process intentionally asks only for a number of items.

## 7. Volumes and scale

_**Must ask. 3 min.** These numbers decide most of your architecture, and they are cheap to ask for and expensive to guess. Twenty records a semester and two hundred thousand a day are different systems._

_Adapt: How many people do you expect at your events? What do you use amount of inventory and other data for? Do you seperate items donated by category?

**What they said:**

ReFrog had seven locations in the prior year and expects eight next year. Most locations need two volunteers, and some need three. The prior event used 193 volunteers for 520 volunteer hours. Wendy estimated about 1,300 shoppers found 6,500 items in 2025 and that 2,573 shoppers found 9,600 items in the most recent year. About 72% of shoppers were students and about 22% were staff, with faculty likely included in the staff category. ReFrog tracks total items in and out, rather than individual inventory; a couch and a notebook each count as one item. Organizers also estimate volume as about 89 eight-yard dumpsters and 21.36 tons diverted in the most recent year.

## 8. Who the users are

_**Must ask. 4 min.** The person who commissions software is often not the person who uses it._

_**If you cannot reach the real users, that is a project risk, not a scheduling detail.** Record it as an `RI-<slug>` the same day. Building from a proxy's account is the most common way a capstone ships something nobody uses, and it is survivable only if you know you are doing it._

_Adapt: Who is going to interact with the refrog app? Who is the primary user for the refrog app?

**What they said:**

The intended app users are donors, shoppers, volunteers, and the three ReFrog administrators: Wendy, Eric, and Courtney. Donors would log what they plan to bring and the location where they will drop it off. Shoppers would verify their TCU affiliation and record the number or type of items they take. Volunteers would sign up for shifts and track their hours and locations. Administrators would use a dashboard or behind-the-scenes data to monitor locations, item activity, volunteer coverage, and possible abuse.

**Can we reach real users? If not, why, and what is the risk:**

The meeting was with Wendy, a ReFrog co-founder and administrator. She named volunteers, donors, and shoppers as real users but did not confirm direct access to them. Risk: their needs and app experience may be inferred through an administrator rather than validated with the people using the app at an event.

## 14. The read-back

_**Never skip. 5 min.** The part teams cut when they run late, and the highest-value five minutes of the hour. Say what you understood in your own words and watch for the correction. A client who is nodding may be being polite; a client correcting you is engaged, and that correction is usually the single most useful sentence of the meeting._

_Read back four things: the problem in one sentence, the objectives with any numbers you got, the top three things you heard are hard, and one thing you believe is **out** of scope. The last produces more correction than the other three together._
